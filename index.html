    How the next question is chosen

    Every new round starts with a cosine-rule bracket, then a sine-rule linkage. Two independent first-check successes on different variations at the working demand unlock more demanding work. A tolerance-only acceptance counts as a success and is labelled separately.

    Support and later rechecks follow an unsuccessful question. Repeated difficulty reduces the working demand without deleting previous achievements. Supported answers count as practice but do not alone unlock harder work. There is no speed penalty.

    Sine and cosine practice are mixed, with untested available variations prioritised. Encountering a template is not mastery. This is the learner’s local device view, not a networked dashboard, an authenticated assessment or a course grade.

    The classroom acceptance band

    Entries and calculated model answers are rounded to two decimal places, with half-hundredths rounded away from zero. Marking then compares exact integer hundredths.

    For a model answer of 24.36, every hundredth from 23.36 to 25.36 inclusive is accepted.
    23.35 and 25.37 are outside the band.

    The tolerance is absolute, not a percentage.
    It means ±1 mm in a millimetre box, ±1 m in a metre box and ±1° for an angle.

    Model matches and tolerance-only acceptances are recorded separately.
    Aim for the model answer to two decimal places.

    This deliberately generous rule is not a manufacturing, surveying or examination tolerance.
    These mathematical models do not establish a structure’s strength, suitability or safety.

    About this resource

    This app develops the supplied oblique-triangle worksheet. It retains its 21 given/asked arrangements and civil-engineering contexts, and adds 15 entry-level and applied templates. Several of the original arrangements deliberately rotate the letters: they are not claimed to be different mathematical laws.

    Questions stay on sine and cosine rules from the start. Additional contexts include mechanical linkages, bracket hole centres, cutting allowances, mixed units, changed opening angles, a shared member between two panels, and a specified obtuse configuration.

    The common-core reference is City & Guilds Design and Development for Engineering and Manufacturing specification v1.5, Unit 300, 4.1, printed page 54. It explicitly includes dimensions of triangles using the sine and cosine rules. Unit 300, 5.1 supports the unit-conversion practice. The scenarios are teaching models, not copied exam items or a complete T Level course.

    All answers are recomputed from the exact given numbers shown to students. Triangle data are validated, including angle ranges and the possibility of a second sine-rule solution. A two-solution template explicitly specifies the obtuse position. Other two-side/one-opposite-angle templates have one valid solution.

    Trigonometric results are generally approximations. The app retains full precision between steps and rounds only final answers to two decimal places. It does not claim that the underlying exact lengths or angles all have terminating decimal expansions.
